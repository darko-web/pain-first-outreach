# Message Frameworks & Channel Rules

## Signal -> Bridge -> CTA

Every message follows this structure regardless of channel.

**Signal** — Open with a pain pattern from VSI signals. This is the most important rule. Trigger events (funding, hiring, launches) tell you *who* to contact and *when* — they are never the hook.

Sources for the Signal opener:
- A pattern across calls: "We've been hearing from a lot of [persona] that X is becoming a real blocker"
- A tension that keeps surfacing: "The question that keeps coming up is..."
- A shared moment of anxiety: "Most [persona] at this stage are wrestling with..."

**Step 1 portability test:** Could you send this message to someone who *hasn't* had the trigger event? If yes, the pain is strong. If no, you've made the trigger the hook — fix it.

**Bridge** — Connect the pain to their specific situation without assuming.
- "Given you're at [stage], I'd guess..."
- "For teams in [context], this usually shows up as..."
- Never: "I'm sure you're experiencing this too" (presumptuous)

**CTA** — One ask. Low friction. Easy to say yes or no. Always discovery-shaped — validate the pain, never sell the solution.
- Good: "Worth 15 mins to compare notes?" / "Curious if this is landing — does this match what you're seeing?" / "Happy to share what we've heard — interested?"
- Bad: "Would you be open to a 30-minute exploratory conversation?" / "Want to see a demo?" / "Open to a pilot?" — solution-mode CTAs break the discovery frame.

## Email rules

**Subject lines:**
- Under 7 words
- Curiosity or specificity, not cleverness
- A/B test 2 variants in Lemlist
- Examples: `"re: [pain topic]"`, `"Quick question about [specific]"`, `"[Company] + [your co]?"`

**Body — Step 1 (cold opener):**
- 3-5 sentences max
- No bullet points (reads as broadcast)
- No links (or one max)
- No product mention, no pitch
- Sign off with just your name

```
[Signal — 1 sentence, specific pain]

[Bridge — 1-2 sentences connecting to their world]

[CTA — 1 sentence, low friction]

[Name]
```

**Step 3 (new angle):**
```
[Callback to step 1 — never "just following up"]

[New angle: a sharper framing of the same pain, OR a question about how they handle it today, OR a different persona's quote on the same pain — never a case study or product proof]

[CTA — still discovery: "is this landing?" / "curious if this matches your reality"]
```

**Breakup email (final step):**
```
[Acknowledge silence without guilt-tripping]

[One last validation question — brief, still about the pain]

[Exit: "Happy to reconnect if timing changes."]
```

## LinkedIn rules

**Connection request character limits:**

| Plan | Limit |
|------|-------|
| LinkedIn Free | 300 characters |
| Premium / Sales Nav (most tiers) | 200 characters |
| Sales Nav Advanced | 300 characters |

Ask the user's plan once per session. Default to **200 chars** if unknown. Count characters before finalizing — trim the least specific part first.

**Connection request rules:**
- Make it about *their* situation, not about you
- Name a specific pain — vague topic references earn nothing
- Never say "no pitch", "no agenda", "just want to connect" — wastes characters, sounds defensive
- Don't ask for their time — earn the connection first, ask in the DM
- Close with a question or observation that creates curiosity

**Good examples (under 200 chars):**
- "Most fintech founders I talk to at your stage have the same doubt: 'do we actually know who we're selling to?' Hit you at a good moment?" (140 chars)
- "Seeing a pattern with SaaS teams at your stage — ICP feels solid until the first sales hire and the motion doesn't transfer. Live question?" (142 chars)

**Bad:** "Working closely with fintech founders on GTM — the ICP question keeps coming up. No pitch, just want to be connected." — vague, about you, disclaimer.

**Follow-up DM (after accept):**
- Wait 24h after acceptance
- 2-3 sentences max
- Reference why you connected
- Don't pitch — open a conversation

```
"Thanks for connecting. [Callback to connection context].

[Signal -> Bridge in 1-2 sentences]

[Soft CTA: "Worth a quick chat?" or "Happy to share more if useful."]"
```

## WhatsApp rules

High-trust, high-attention channel. Tier A only, Step 5+.

- Max 3 sentences
- Conversational tone — reads like a text from a colleague
- No links, no attachments
- Must reference prior contact ("Following up from my emails...")
- Easy opt-out always

```
"Hey [Name], [Your name] here from [Co]. Following up on a few emails — I know things get busy.

[Signal in 1 sentence — conversational]

[Soft CTA] — happy to leave it there if not relevant right now."
```

## Campaign type archetypes

### Cold
- Step 1: Signal opener -> bridge -> curiosity CTA
- Step 2: LinkedIn connection (reference signal, don't repeat email)
- Step 3: Sharper version of the pain, or a question about how they're handling it today
- Step 4: LinkedIn DM -> new angle on the same pain
- Step 5: WhatsApp (Tier A only)
- Step 6: Breakup email — one last validation question, no guilt

### Warm re-engagement
- Step 1: Acknowledge prior contact, lead with what's changed
- Step 2: LinkedIn DM with new angle
- Step 3: New angle on the original pain — a fresh question or recent pattern from other calls (no case studies, no product reveals)

### Event-triggered
The trigger tells you *who* and *when*. The pain is still the opener.
- Step 1: Pain pattern characteristic of people in this trigger moment
- Step 2: LinkedIn connection — no reference to trigger, no pitch
- Step 3: Value-add email — new angle
- Step 4: LinkedIn DM — soft conversation opener

### ABM (Account-Based)
- Research the specific account first
- Step 1: Account-specific signal -> bridge -> CTA
- Each step: new account-level insight
- Coordinate LinkedIn + email on same account in parallel
- All steps stay in discovery mode — account-specific signals validate the pain, never pitch the solution

## Common mistakes

- **Trigger as hook**: "Congrats on the raise" — no value, no pain, no reason to reply
- **Flattery opener**: "Came across your impressive work at..." — skipped immediately
- **Wall of text**: >100 words in step 1 = not read
- **Following up on following up**: "Just bumping this" — delete
- **Vague CTA**: "Let me know your thoughts" — on what?
- **Same angle repeated**: Each step needs a new reason to respond
- **"No pitch" disclaimers**: Everyone knows you have an agenda. Disclaiming it wastes space.
- **Pitching the solution (even softly)**: Breaks the discovery frame. Every message validates the pain — no demos, case studies, or "here's what we built." Late-step variation comes from new pain framings, not solution reveals.
